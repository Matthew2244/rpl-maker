Plug-ins REAPER hasn't scanned yet, and IK Multimedia's instruments. Libraries now come out for Hammond B-3X, MODO BASS 2, MODO DRUM, Pianoverse and SampleTank 4 from their NKS presets.

- Magpie finds your plug-ins through REAPER's own list, and REAPER only adds a plug-in to it when it starts. A plug-in installed since then was missing from Magpie. Now Look for Presets Again names any plug-in like that.
- New: File, Scan New Plug-ins. The checking copy of REAPER scans those plug-ins, and they show up like the rest. Your REAPER and its list aren't changed. A big instrument can take a minute or more to scan. On a Mac, if macOS asks whether REAPER for checking presets can open a folder, choose Allow. Libraries made this way work in your REAPER once it has scanned the plug-in too, which it does the next time it starts. From the command line it's `rplmaker scan-new`.
- Some plug-ins take a moment to apply a preset after it's chosen. Read back straight away, they still hold the last one, so every preset failed. When most presets fail, Magpie now tries a few again with a second to settle each, and checks the whole set that way when that's what was wrong. Hammond B-3X needs this. Those plug-ins take longer to check.
- Magpie can now read the settings IK Multimedia's plug-ins save, so their presets are checked setting by setting instead of by their controls. Window size, panel layout and the plug-in's build number don't count as settings.
- A preset saved by an older version of a plug-in now goes in under the version you have installed, when that's the only difference.
- When a preset is left out because a block of data changed, the report says how big the block is instead of reading out the raw data.
- A plug-in REAPER runs through its Intel bridge no longer has "(x86_64)" in its library's name.
